What it is for

However small, however odd, somebody has met it before

Every dot behind the wordmark is a person — some of them electricians, plumbers, builders — and the lines are what appears between them when one of them speaks up. Nobody is asking us. They are talking to each other.

A room for every trade

Electricians. Plumbers. Heating and gas. Builders and renovation. Landscapers. Roofers. Painters and decorators. Tech and wifi. Walk into the room, ask, and somebody who does it for a living answers — identity checked, listed at the top.

Groups you start yourselves

A Sunday book club. A bike club. Three people who want to walk on Tuesdays and begin something. Name it, say what it is for, and it is yours to run.

Groups already running

Whatever the people nearby have started. Cooking, cycling, reading, walking — and whatever is started next week that nobody has thought of yet.

Company, on any terms

Say hello at 3am. Walk round the park slowly. Take a seat at somebody's table on Sunday. Say one small thing about your day so the week does not pass unnoticed.

Fourteen rooms to begin with, and as many more as people start. What goes on in them is not fourteen things — it is whatever the people nearby happen to know, and whatever they decide to do together.

Trust

Built around what can go wrong

Somebody is coming to your door. That shapes most of the decisions here.

Professionals are checkedAnybody who answers in a trade room, lists a trade or hosts a get-together has had their identity confirmed by a person. Asking never requires it.
What the badge meansThat a real, contactable person is behind the account — not that they are a good plumber. Their reviews are on their page; the ones about paid work say they happened off Commons.
Trade rooms are curatedAnyone can start a book club. A room called Plumbers is a claim about who answers in it, so those are set up by moderators.
No addressesA get-together has no location field. The host tells each guest privately once they say they are coming.
Real accounts onlyEmail, phone and username, each confirmed with a code. One account per address and per number, which is most of what stops a spammer opening fifty.
No documents keptAn identity check records that it happened, when, and how. The document itself is never uploaded and never stored.
Reports get readThree reports hide something. A moderator then rules on it, and that ruling holds.
