What it is for

However small, however odd, somebody has met it before

A radiator that will not heat. A form nobody can read. A Saturday with nothing in it. Every dot behind the wordmark is a person, and the lines are what appears between them when one of them needs a hand.

Around the house

Leaks, damp, dead sockets, boilers, drains, draughts, a door that will not shut, a bill that looks wrong, a quote you are not sure about, a tool you need for one afternoon.

Outside and online

Whatever is eating the tomatoes, a lawn going bald, a tree too close to the wall, wifi that drops at seven, a phone nobody can explain, a printer that has never once worked.

Things worth belonging to

Cooking, baking, sewing, woodwork, books, new babies, mending what is broken instead of replacing it. Join one, or start the one that is missing.

Company, on any terms

Say hello at 3am. Walk round the park slowly. Take a seat at somebody's table on Sunday. Say one small thing about your day so the week does not pass unnoticed.

Six categories, because a short list is easier to read than a long one. What goes in them is not six things — it is whatever the people nearby happen to know, which is more than any list would hold.

How it goes

You askIn plain words. What is happening, and what you have tried.
Somebody answersA person nearby who has done it, and whose identity has been checked.
You say what workedIt goes to the top, for the next person with the same problem.

Trust

Built around what can go wrong

Somebody is coming to your door. That shapes most of the decisions here.

Helpers are checkedAnybody who answers a question, lists a trade or hosts a get-together has had their identity confirmed by a person. Asking for help never requires it.
What the badge meansThat a real, contactable person is behind the account — not that they are a good plumber. Reviews of paid work happened off Commons and say so.
No addressesA get-together has no location field. The host tells each guest privately once they say they are coming.
Real accounts onlyEmail, phone and username, each confirmed with a code. One account per address and per number, which is most of what stops a spammer opening fifty.
No documents keptAn identity check records that it happened, when, and how. The document itself is never uploaded and never stored.
Reports get readThree reports hide something. A moderator then rules on it, and that ruling holds.
